## Short description

Earth goes quiet. Six crew need a plan. In this Mars game for ages 7–12, players predict, prepare, investigate and manage a small outpost, then replay one decision to compare two real simulated futures. NASA elevation and NASA/JPL light-time data connect the choices to science. An anonymous learning card records the player's reasoning, observations and result.

## The problem and our approach

A list of space facts does not show why life-support systems depend on each other. BLACKOUT turns those connections into a short, guided mission. Children see Air, Water and Power first, with other resources and explanations available. Deliberate Next day turns make reading time safe. A mistake can stop work or exhaust a supply, but the player can recover and try a different plan. The target is an 8–12-minute guided session; timing and understanding still need a real child pilot.

The distinctive feature is **one mission, two futures**: replay the same starting colony, outside events and later requested actions with one preparation changed. The comparison uses the real game rules, preserves the original attempt and identifies any replay action that becomes invalid. Players then explain the result and apply the idea to a new situation. This is a testable educational design, not a claim of measured learning gains.

## Implemented experience

- A complete nine-day training scenario with six adult crew, limited Parts and workers, a delayed Earth reply, a radio quiet period, separate dust and radiation exercises, a consequential ending and recovery.
- Battery storage and solar alignment plus maintenance are viable strategies. Waiting conserves Parts but requires thoughtful maintenance; no choice guarantees safety regardless of later actions.
- Optional first-person dosimeter fieldwork with an equivalent map route. Readings are explicitly simulated relative indices.
- Interactive Air, Water and Power systems inspection linked to actual inputs, outputs and stopped-system reasons.
- Versioned mission codes, safety-first results, separate first-attempt/practice records and finite Fame rewards. Local records are useful for a classroom, not a tamper-proof online leaderboard.
- Refined Blender assets, state-linked machinery, articulated suited adult crew, five no-gold themes, quiet original audio, captions, mute, keyboard controls and readable-text settings.
- A self-contained offline game, downloadable anonymous learning record, printable evidence card and teacher/pilot kit. The longer Junior campaign and Commander mode remain available.

## NASA evidence and its limits

**MOLA:** NASA-derived elevation informs landing-site relief. Local details are embellished for play; ice likelihood and caves are modeled regional rules, not surveyed deposits.

**JPL Horizons:** the bundled Earth–Mars record for 20 November 2023 supplies a one-way light time of 20.98606 minutes. The displayed approximate round trip is twice that value, 41.97212 minutes. The training clock compresses 20 radio minutes into one game day. A scheduled command pause is distinct from darkness or electrical failure. The historical reference does not claim a live forecast.

**Life support, solar power and radiation:** NASA ISS water recovery, MOXIE, solar-orientation and mission accounts explain the mechanisms. Game production rates, costs, boost percentages, crop times, event scheduling and shelter indices are disclosed simplifications. RadLab, OSDR and SWIM datasets have not been integrated into this release. Existing DONKI material is Earth space weather, not a direct Mars forecast.

The source panels connect observation → simplified rule → player decision → consequence. NASA does not endorse this game. There is no claim of NASA-grade dose calculation, a physical Mars digital twin, UE5 rendering or ray tracing.

## Testing and learning evidence

The release process runs the authoritative game engine across multiple seeds, sites and policies, plus complete browser playthroughs using visible controls. It tests comparison isolation, finite spending/rewards, save migration, corrupt-save rejection, pending messages, recovery, first-person movement, accessibility and offline operation. The release report records exact counts, artifacts and limitations separately from this draft.

**Child pilot not yet conducted.** The ready-to-use kit records first independent action, help requested, a stopped-system explanation and a transfer answer without collecting names. Actual child understanding, enjoyment, session duration and classroom-device performance remain to be evaluated. Automated tests are not evidence of learning gains.
