# How AI was used in this project

The NASA Space Apps Challenge calls AI tools "Super Intelligence (SI)" and asks teams to say how they were used.

The team chose the challenge, Mars-colony concept, audience, visual references and intended learning goals, and directed revisions. AI tools helped propose refinements as well as implement code, art, tests and documentation. The team reviews the resulting product and submission; the project does not represent all implementation or wording as exclusively human-authored.

## What was not generated

- The Mars image, the height data, the storm records and the four detailed 3D models are NASA material. See `NOTICE`.
- The bundled fonts and the 3D library are open-source work by other people. See `NOTICE`.

## Marks inside the project

- Original game and authoring sources include AI-assistance disclosures; unmodified third-party code retains its own attribution.
- The page carries a `generator` note in its head.
- The game's Credits screen states how AI was used.
- The current human mission adviser is generated with Grok Imagine and labeled as fictional AI artwork. The earlier Martian portrait is retained as legacy artwork. Neither portrays an actual NASA employee or scientific evidence. No generated video is used.
- No NASA logo, flag or mission identifier is used. The ones on the NASA models were painted over.

## Ares Atlas and Junior mode, 14 November 2026

OpenAI Codex assisted with the original Ares interface, simplified Junior rules,
browser tests, authored 3D building geometry, procedural suit motion and surface
materials. Blender was controlled through the official Blender Lab MCP server to
author and export custom buildings. The team selected the visual direction from
concepts made with Grok Imagine; a new Grok portrait is included in the game with
its provenance in `game/assets/ares-ui/ARTWORK.md`.

The NASA MOLA terrain remains the scientific map input. Fine regolith detail,
buildings, character art and gameplay resource units are illustrative or invented.
Junior crop times, automatic work shifts, storm timing and checkpoint recovery are
training rules, not physical predictions. Scripted tests check behavior and viable
paths; they do not establish child-tested difficulty, learning outcomes or AAA
production quality.

## Full surface kit and water/crew revision

Codex assisted with the Blender surface kit, articulated suit and lighter models, vehicles, NASA-derived Blender terrain export, water mass balance, individual role allocation, UI and tests. The Grok-generated regolith colour is used unaltered as artistic material detail; its generation is recorded in NOTICE. Blender Cycles generated the actual-model preview images. Three.js screen-space ambient occlusion is an approximation, not hardware ray tracing. UE5 project/import source was prepared but has not been run in Unreal; no Lumen render or native game port is claimed.

## DEAD RECKONING: BLACKOUT revision

The team renamed the game and requested five interface alternatives without gold, a human astronaut adviser, useful first-person field work, generator boosts and Fame. Codex assisted with the code, testing and original design. Grok Imagine generated the new human adviser; provenance is recorded in NOTICE. NASA source facts are separated from teaching values and simulated radiation readings.

## Overnight Blackout mission and automated playtesting

OpenAI Codex, directed by the team's approved build plan, implemented the short mission, exact-state replay, evidence/competition records, save validation, systems inspector, local synthesized audio, accessibility work, automated policy/browser players and documentation. Codex also proposed refinements and reviewed their educational/causal limits. The team approved the plan before execution.

Four original Blender service props and improved procedural crew/world presentation were authored using AI-assisted Python and the installed Blender background process. Existing Grok-generated terrain colour and fictional adult adviser imagery are reused. A visible in-game label identifies generated artwork; exported game photos and the demonstration preserve a visible disclosure. No new synthetic child imagery or voice is used.

The demonstration records the actual game and uses editorial captions; it is not a generated simulation of a finished product. Locally synthesized WebAudio uses original oscillator/noise code, not a recording of a person. NASA input records remain sourced observations/calculations, not AI-generated data. Automated tests do not establish human learning outcomes.

## Student-selected cinematic release, 15 November 2026

Students selected Cinematic Expedition (welcome 1), Field Scientist (equipment 2),
and Two Futures (film 6). The welcome uses the selected ChatGPT-generated art
with real HTML controls. The detailed first-person glove is a transparent image
derived from the selected art through OpenAI image generation, with live HTML
readings and motion. It is a 2.5D view model, not a newly rigged 3D hand.

Film 6 combines the team's existing Grok-generated cinematics and actual game
captures. Hades narration is the previously generated Inworld stock voice; it is
not a student or cloned human voice. Codex translated the approved narration to
Modern Standard Arabic and retimed the edit against the Hades recording. Arabic
captions are burned into the video for reliable browser/fullscreen playback;
editable SRT/VTT sidecars are included. The original synthesized music/effects
are mixed beneath one continuous voice recording. No scientific input data were
changed by this presentation release.
