# How AI was used in this project

The NASA Space Apps Challenge calls AI tools "Super Intelligence (SI)" and asks teams to say how they were used.

**The idea and the design of this game are the team's own.** The team chose the challenge, the concept, the audience,
the rules of the game and what it should teach. AI tools were used to speed up the building.

## What was generated, and with which tool

| What | Tool | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Game code (`game/index.html`) | Claude (Anthropic) | Written from the team's design document and directed by the team in review rounds. |
| Lesson text, Mission Control problems, fact card wording | Claude (Anthropic) | Drafted from the team's design. Facts carry source links to NASA and partner pages. |
| Helmet view, Space weather panel, surface sample choice (`game/expedition.js`, `game/expedition.css`) | AI coding tools, directed by a team member | Merged into the game on 14 November 2026. |
| Helper scripts (`tools/`) | Claude (Anthropic) | |
| Project documents (`README.md`, `docs/`, this file) | Claude (Anthropic) | |
| Building shapes that are not NASA models | Claude (Anthropic) | Simple shapes drawn in code. |

## What was not generated

- The Mars image, the height data, the storm records and the four detailed 3D models are NASA material. See `NOTICE`.
- The two fonts and the 3D library are open-source work by other people. See `NOTICE`.

## Marks inside the project

- Every code file starts with a comment that says it was generated with AI.
- The page carries a `generator` note in its head.
- The game's Credits screen states how AI was used.
- The fictional Martian guide portrait is generated with Grok Imagine and visibly labeled as AI artwork in the interface. It is a story character, not evidence of life on Mars. No generated video is used.
- No NASA logo, flag or mission identifier is used. The ones on the NASA models were painted over.

## Ares Atlas and Junior mode, 14 November 2026

OpenAI Codex assisted with the original Ares interface, simplified Junior rules,
browser tests, authored 3D building geometry, procedural suit motion and surface
materials. Blender was controlled through the official Blender Lab MCP server to
author and export custom buildings. The team selected the visual direction from
concepts made with Grok Imagine; a new Grok portrait is included in the game with
its provenance in `game/assets/ares-ui/ARTWORK.md`.

The NASA MOLA terrain remains the scientific map input. Fine regolith detail,
buildings, character art and gameplay resource units are illustrative or invented.
Junior crop times, automatic work shifts, storm timing and checkpoint recovery are
training rules, not physical predictions. Scripted tests check behavior and viable
paths; they do not establish child-tested difficulty, learning outcomes or AAA
production quality.
