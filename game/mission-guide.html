One mission, two futures

Choose Blackout mission to begin in a working outpost with six adult crew, 36 air, 36 food, 30 water and 12 Parts. Predict a useful preparation, then choose a battery, solar alignment or keeping your Parts. Each plan uses the real colony rules.

  1. Predict and prepare. A battery stores surplus energy; alignment temporarily improves generation. Later maintenance can also matter.
  2. Investigate. Compare open-ground and shelter markers with the dosimeter or the map alternative. The 100/30 readings are a simulated relative index, not a real radiation dose. Choose a shelter.
  3. Plan while Earth is quiet. The NASA/JPL row for 20 November 2023 gives 20.98606 minutes one-way light time. A reply is approximated as twice that. Each training day advances the radio clock by 20 minutes; this is not the length of a Mars day. The scheduled radio pause delays advice; separately scheduled dust reduces solar generation.
  4. Observe and revise. Advance deliberately through day 9. A shortage creates a recoverable setback. Compare a different preparation with the same starting state and recorded later actions, then explain the result.

The first attempt is kept separately from practice. The mission code fixes the setup and events; everyone starts with the same six-person obligation. Reading speed never changes rank. Download the anonymous learning record or print its evidence card. Local results are not a tamper-proof online leaderboard.

Systems opens an interactive schematic of the actual air, water and power state. Rock-sample collection stays in the longer campaigns so it cannot change a short comparison outside its recorded decisions.

Continue with the Junior campaign

Choose Junior campaign for the longer guided colony-building experience. You start with six adult explorers, 96 air, 144 food, 64 water and 68 Parts. Your first job is to build solar power.

The map waits while you think. Next day gives the crew one day of work, grows powered crops, updates supplies and moves events forward. A short work animation shows the result; it does not spend another day.

  1. Catch sunlight. Build a solar panel.
  2. Make air. Produce enough oxygen for everyone.
  3. Recover water and grow food. Power a Water Processor, then allow your greenhouses to grow.
  4. Welcome two explorers. Prepare supplies and beds before inviting them.
  5. Prepare for dust. Store spare power and clean dusty panels.
  6. Take shelter. Protect everyone before the radiation practice alert.
  7. Explore. Send a rover while the people at home keep the colony supplied.

Finishing the guided mission does not end your colony. Keep building, invite more people when you can support them, or begin a separate Commander mission.

Water: reuse is different from extraction

The landing pod recovers 75% of crew water and 50% of plant water. A powered, staffed Water Processor improves these to 95% and 90%. It works on ordinary ground; a likely-ice tile also allows it to extract 4 new water/day.

Try the numbers. Eight people and two greenhouses use 9 water/day before recycling. A processor on ordinary ground recovers 8.5, so the reserve falls by 0.5. Put it on likely ice and it also extracts 4, so the reserve rises by 3.5.

Extra ordinary-ground processors do not multiply recycling beyond the recovery fraction. Water is conserved.

A supply shuttle brings 8 Parts and 12 water every eight days, starting on day 9. A colony that only recycles still uses water top-ups. A processor on ice can replace that imported water.

Power, fieldwork and Fame

Give sunlight a better angle

Open Power → Align solar arrays in Junior. The kit costs 2 Parts and takes one technician day. The technician comes from available people after essential machine operators are assigned. Building work can slow while that person aligns the arrays.

After that work day, solar generation rises by 20% for the next three production days. The landing pod and reactor receive no bonus. Dust in the sky and dirt on the panels still reduce solar output. You cannot stack alignments or start another until eight days after the previous booking. A radiation alert or unavailable technician delays the work; switching off every solar array also pauses it.

This is a short engineering choice, not a new research tree. NASA explains that solar output depends on the angle to sunlight; Orion's arrays can turn to follow the Sun. The 20%, three-day window, cost and cooldown are invented game tuning. They are not measured Mars-colony performance. Commander keeps its existing upgrades, research and cleaning instead of this timed action.

Compare a real observation. InSight recovered about 30 watt-hours per Martian day after wind-blown grains removed some solar-panel dust on 22 May 2021. This was cleaning, not a universal percentage boost or Sun-tracking result. Read the NASA/JPL report.

Measure a difference, then explain it

In Walk mode, the held instrument shows a simulated relative exposure index. Record open ground, the labelled rock comparison site and the marker beside a completed shelter. Compare different conditions. The display is an educational model, not a calibrated dosimeter or a real-time NASA measurement. Its shelter reduction is a game assumption; an archived measured event dose is labelled separately.

Fame rewards evidence, not repeated clicking

The Fame counter records achievements in your current mission save. Each Junior objective earns 3 Fame. The first successful solar alignment earns 4. The first rover survey of each terrain kind earns 3; “likely ice” remains a map estimate, not a confirmed deposit. Your first open, rock and shelter readings each earn 3. Comparing open ground with rock earns 4 more; comparing open ground with shelter earns another 4.

Repeating an action, measuring the same condition on another day or returning to the same kind of ground gives no extra evidence award. Saves keep the award ledger. Checkpoint retry restores both Fame and evidence to that checkpoint, so replay cannot accumulate extra rewards.

Medals begin at 20, 50, 100, 170 and 260 Fame. Junior Fame celebrates learning; it does not gate air, water, food, buildings or invitations. Commander keeps its existing achievements, evidence-project rewards and penalties; its medals improve the colony's share of shared passengers. New field evidence adds to that same score without resetting it.

One person, one role

Population is your actual head count. More people give you more hands, and they also need more air, food, water, beds and shelter. Junior assigns everyday jobs automatically so you can focus on the colony plan.

  1. Explorers leave the home workforce. A rover carries two free people. They cannot also run machines at home.
  2. Operators keep essential systems running. Each enabled machine gets one operator, in this order: air, water, food, reactor, mine, recreation. Solar panels and batteries do not need a continuous operator.
  3. A booked alignment uses one technician. The technician comes after machine operators and before other available jobs. One person cannot operate a machine and align arrays at once.
  4. Other free people build or clean. Up to two builders provide 2 work each/day. Cleaning takes priority over construction for that day.
  5. Everyone else rests. Resting people are available capacity. During a radiation alert, the people at home shelter and do no production work.

Example: Eight people operating two oxygen machines, one Water Processor and two greenhouses means five operators. Two others can build, leaving one resting. With no building queued, those three rest. Sending two of them on a rover leaves one free at home.

The Crew panel gives the exact role totals and names. Map figures show people doing those jobs; they are not extra population. The game does not simulate every walk, traffic jam or travel-time cost between buildings. A powered-off machine releases its operator. A machine merely waiting for power keeps its assigned operator until you switch it off.

Where do arriving people go?

In Junior, you invite them. The colony must have enough beds and sustained air/food output for the expanded crew, two days of air/food reserves, water after recycling, and enough operators for enabled machines. Two people arrive next day. If readiness falls after booking, they wait safely in orbit. They immediately join the work roster when they land.

The first pair brings 4 Parts once. Later pairs need additional capacity and do not repeat the bonus. Junior does not send your invited explorers to a competing colony.

Appeal tells you how the colony is doing

Appeal is a readable condition report in Junior: 40 base points, plus secure air (15), secure food (15), a healthy water reserve (10), beds (5), shelter (5), sufficient power and crew (5), and recreation for everyone (5). Open it to see which conditions are ready. A shortage recovery caps the score at 35.

Appeal does not secretly change Junior invitations. In Commander, appeal also influences where shared shuttle passengers choose to live.

Choices have consequences

Dust interrupts sunlight

The guided forecast gives five days to prepare. The storm lasts three days and cuts solar generation to 35%. Batteries and reserves keep the colony going. Afterward, panels remain at 70% cleanliness until a free crew member can clean them; construction waits that day.

Radiation needs shelter

The practice alert gives five days' warning. Enough shelter means one day under cover. Too little means a safe retreat to the landing ship: two days without work and 2 Parts spent on transport. Stored supplies matter while machines stop.

Shortage means revise and retry

If air, food or water runs out, Junior stops time and offers a checkpoint retry. Progress since the last completed objective is undone, including spending and elapsed days. Earlier achievements remain, and the retry count is recorded. No colonists die or disappear in Junior.

Power shortages are real: optional machines switch off before life-support machines. Too many enabled machines for the crew leaves some unstaffed. A good response might be another solar array, a charged battery, switching off recreation temporarily, or waiting to grow the population.

Exploration uses time and people

A rover costs 1 Part and two free people. Choose unclaimed ground 3–10 hexes from the pod. It surveys nearby ground in two days and returns in four. It cannot take operators away from essential machines without your first freeing them.

The dust and radiation timetable, costs and recovery rules are teaching scenarios. The weather panel's dated observations are not a live hazard forecast for your colony.

Commander: the deeper simulation

Commander keeps the earlier, longer strategy rules. It is optional and is not the recommended starting point for seven-year-olds. Its harder systems are real changes to the economy, not simply more information on screen.

Production, people and maintenance

Parts and metal both pay for construction. Each operator reserves one person; the remaining available people build. Rover trips and specialist duties reduce that pool. Greenhouses normally need 20 powered sols, reduced to 12 by the relevant technology.

Six named founders have engineering, growing or power skills. A specialist leaves general work. Crop research can improve yield, power tuning can lower consumption, repairs need assigned labour, and training takes 12 working sols per level up to level 3. New arrivals join the general pool.

Equipment condition reduces output below 70%; zero condition stops it. Repair costs 2 Parts and 1 metal, takes the machine offline once work starts, and requires labour. Distance and NASA-derived terrain height change the invented repair-work requirement. Repairing a battery can discard charge above the remaining storage capacity.

Commander retains its earlier water abstraction: an Ice Melter waters nearby production capacity and produces air. It does not use Junior's new stored-water ledger.

Shared passengers, rivals, appeal and trade

A passenger shuttle arrives every ten sols outside blackout, usually carrying two or three people, or six during a launch window. The three colonies compete for the same passengers. Each eligible colony's weight is its appeal raised to 1.5; your Fame medals improve your weight. Allocation uses a nominal 60% share cap before whole-person rounding, with at least one place for eligible colonies when possible.

Your pad must be open and appeal at least 40. Air/food reserves, shelter, recreation, employment, beds, crowding and industrial noise affect appeal. Very low appeal can cause someone to move to a rival; retirement also reduces population. Arrivals bring equipment, and periodic supply ships bring Parts. This is distinct from Junior's explicit invitation.

Rivals have finite Parts/metal budgets and one building queue. Their chosen facilities raise recruiting appeal. Their complete air/food economy is not simulated. Trading gives you 3 Parts in exchange for 6 metal and changes their real budget; there is a 30-sol cooldown.

Evidence projects, Earth communications and research

Optional Council projects ask you to prove sustainable air, food or shelter reserves for three consecutive sols. You commit a 2-Part bond, collect evidence and send it to Earth before a deadline. Success returns the bond and adds 6 Parts and 6 Fame. Missing or cancelling loses the bond and 5 Fame, and gives a rival +8 recruitment appeal for 30 sols.

A saved NASA/JPL Horizons calculation supplies the Earth–Mars light time for the displayed date. A reply is approximated as twice one-way time and compressed into whole game sols. A scripted conjunction blackout pauses communication. Signal travel is different from the separate six-sol orbital supply-crate rule.

Technology choices can improve crops, oxygen, shielding or power systems. Choosing an upgrade rather than immediate supplies is an opportunity cost. Quizzes, event questions, the journal and source explanations supplement the simulation; correct answers are not proof that the colony is sustainable.

Hazards, independence and the long campaign

Commander includes dust, radiation decisions, equipment wear, low reserves, population departures, festivals, crop problems, cargo orders and solar conjunction. Choosing to work during radiation exposes people; sheltering stops production. Emergency supplies and later evacuations/departures replace Junior's checkpoint system.

Independence slowly increases when reserves and power are healthy and falls when the colony needs help. The victory condition requires 100% independence and at least one successfully survived conjunction blackout. An abandoned colony ends the campaign.

This mode's full-campaign balance remains an open issue. Existing automated policies struggled with maintenance and long campaigns. The guided Junior mode has a tested completion path; that result should not be read as proof that Commander is balanced for children.

A ten-minute classroom investigation

  1. Minutes 0–2 · Predict. Ask, “What does a greenhouse need besides the building?” Have the learner choose a prediction: power, people, water or time.
  2. Minutes 2–5 · Build and observe. Start a mission or continue a prepared save. Inspect a resource, place a needed building, and advance one day at a time. Read the stock and daily change aloud.
  3. Minutes 5–7 · Test a change. Switch off an optional machine or compare water before and after a processor. Predict the result before advancing.
  4. Minutes 7–9 · Explain. Ask, “Why did that number change?” and “What would two more people need?” Open the matching NASA source explanation.
  5. Minute 9–10 · Revise and save. Choose one improvement and explain it. Save locally for the next session.

Observe whether the learner can make a prediction, identify a dependency and revise a plan. A winning script does not establish that a child understood the science. Allow reading aloud and shared decisions; do not turn every action into a quiz.

How the design evolved

The original trainer already had air, food, power, buildings, anonymous workers, shared shuttles, storms and a long independence campaign. The strategy upgrades connected these systems more deeply: site forecasts, evidence contracts, communication delay, named specialists, production methods, repairs and rival development.

Junior then removed the burdens that did not suit a first lesson. It keeps placement, dependencies, growth decisions and preparation. It replaces continuous waiting with deliberate days, twenty-day crops with four growing days, metal plus Parts with one construction price, shared recruitment with invitations, and permanent losses with checkpoint retries.

The current update adds a real water ledger, a conserved recovery model, exact automatic crew roles, explained Appeal, a timed solar-alignment tradeoff and one-time field-evidence Fame. Names identify people and their work; there is no deep relationship or dynasty simulation. Commander retains the advanced management systems for players who want them.

Science you can check

The scientific sources are real. Building prices, production units, crop times, shelter capacity, shuttles and event schedules are game rules. NASA does not endorse DEAD RECKONING: BLACKOUT.

  • NASA Planetary Data System · MOLA — measured planetary heights used by the terrain model.
  • NASA · MOXIE results — demonstrated oxygen extraction from the Martian atmosphere.
  • NASA · Water-recovery milestone — the station demonstrated recovery of 98% of collected water in its U.S. system. Junior uses simpler 75%/95% crew rates.
  • NASA · Veggie — plant research and supplemental fresh food in space. Four-day Martian harvests are fictional.
  • NASA · SWIM water-ice map — evidence about subsurface ice. Current game ice colours are a latitude estimate, not this measured raster.
  • NASA/JPL · Radiation measured at Mars — motivates shelter planning; the game does not calculate a medically valid astronaut dose.
  • NASA · Solar power principles — solar-array orientation changes output; the game bonus is invented.
  • NASA · Orion solar arrays — arrays rotate on two axes to remain Sun-aligned; these are spacecraft, not deployed Mars-colony designs.
  • NASA/JPL · Horizons — dated Earth–Mars light times used by the short mission and Commander's evidence messages.
