Hello, explorer! In case you were wondering how time is flying by so fast on Mars while it's relatively slow on your end, the answer is: you're hovering 6.6 meters above Sagittarius A*, the supermassive black hole at the center of our galaxy.

Einstein's general relativity says strong gravity slows down time. Your clock is compared to Mars's like this:

your time = Mars time × √(1 − rs/r)

Here rs is the black hole's event horizon (about 12.7 million km) and r is how far you are from its center. You're so close to the edge that the square root comes out to about 1/44,000, so your clock runs 44,000 times slower than the clocks on Mars. One minute for you is one month on Mars.

Real fact: this works on a tiny scale too. Mars has weaker gravity than Earth, so clocks there really do run 477 millionths of a second faster every day.
